Research

I work on machine learning for the sciences. The domains I have worked in look unrelated on paper: the atmosphere, language models, ceramic manufacturing, molecules, road networks. But the problem underneath has been the same each time. Scientific data rarely arrives in the form a model wants, and most of the work is finding a representation in which the problem becomes tractable.

At KAIST I work on weather and climate. The atmosphere is a continuous field, but we only ever see it through station readings, radar sweeps, satellite images, and reanalysis grids, each at a different resolution and in a different format. I represent the field implicitly, as a neural function of space and time, so that heterogeneous observations can be fused and the forecast can be queried at any resolution. This is the basis of the regional downscaling and generative dynamics models I lead in CLIMECAST, aimed at kilometer-scale forecasting over the Korean Peninsula.

I came to this from optimization. Many scientific problems reduce to searching an enormous discrete space for inputs that score well on an expensive objective, such as a molecule with the right binding affinity or a process setting that yields a better ceramic. Bayesian optimization handles expensive objectives but not discrete spaces, so the approach is to learn a continuous latent space and search there. That only works if the latent space is faithful to the objective, and my Ph.D. developed methods that shape it for exactly this: regularizing it so that distance tracks objective value, inverting the decoder so that the search stays where the model is reliable, and using normalizing flows so that proposals remain valid. The same machinery turned out to optimize prompts for black-box language models, which was a useful reminder that the method was never about molecules.

Before that I worked on graph neural networks, mostly data augmentation, meta-path learning on heterogeneous graphs, and hyperbolic embeddings. That is where I first learned that the representation is usually the problem.

The domain will probably change again. The question of what space to work in will not.
